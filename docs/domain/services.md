# Services

The service range can be broad, but each offer should explain the client problem, scope, deliverables, and limits. Do not invent agency capacity. Public copy should lead with the offer and boundaries, not with headcount. All offers below are **PROPOSED** until the owner confirms capability and availability.

## 1. Websites and web experiences
For businesses that need a clear, credible online presence or a better customer journey.
- May include discovery, information architecture, responsive UI/UX, implementation, forms, integrations, deployment, and handover.
- Clarify whether copywriting, branding, SEO, analytics, CMS setup, and ongoing maintenance are included per project.

## 2. Web applications and business tools
Custom web apps, dashboards, portals, workflows, and integrations where off-the-shelf tools are insufficient.
- Scope, security, data migration, hosting, and support must be agreed per project.

## 3. MVP and product development
Help turn a defined idea into a testable first version.
- May include scope definition, user flows, UI/UX, implementation, deployment, and a plan for learning from early users.
- Do not promise market validation, adoption, or commercial success.

## 4. Mobile development
Mobile applications or mobile-friendly experiences where the business case justifies them.
- Confirm supported platforms, release responsibilities, app-store accounts, device testing, and ongoing maintenance before quoting.

## 5. UX/UI design
Improve usability and clarify user journeys before or alongside implementation.
- May include requirements workshops, user flows, wireframes, interface design, and implementation-ready specifications.
- Do not claim formal user research or usability testing unless it is actually performed.

## 6. Software consulting and engineering review
Focused help with architecture, code quality, performance, maintainability, technical choices, or delivery risks.
- Define the review scope, access, findings format, and whether implementation is included.

## General boundaries
- Do not advertise a technology, certification, service level, fixed timeline, or guarantee without confirmation.
- Prefer a clear primary offer and a small number of supporting offers over an undifferentiated list of “everything.”
